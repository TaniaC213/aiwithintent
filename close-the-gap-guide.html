way 1 of 6

One system, not five apps

If your business knowledge is scattered across five different apps, a doc here, a note there, a folder you forgot about, Claude can't use any of it properly, no matter how good the tool is.

the ai brain

A system, not a single folder

Anthropic's own research names this as one of three things separating businesses that get real value from AI and those that don't: reliable data in one place.

That comes from setting up a system of folders organised around how your business actually works, department by department, so Claude always knows exactly where to look.

Your AI Brain/
├──About the Business.md← who you are, what you do, who it's for
├──Voice Profile.md← how you write, words you'd never use
├──skills/← skills that apply across the whole business
├──brand/← logo, colours, fonts, style guide
├──offers/← what you sell, your packages, your pricing
├──clients/
│   └──client-name/
│      ├──About This Client.md← context specific to this one client, outputs for them saved right here too
│      └──skills/← skills that only apply to this client
├──content/← emails, social posts, drafts, lead magnets, and anything Claude creates for this area
└──operations/← processes, templates, how you work

Skills don't all belong in one place either. A skill that applies across your whole business, your standard email format, your brand voice, sits in the top-level skills/ folder. A skill that only makes sense for one client or project, exactly how they like their reports formatted, lives inside that client's own folder instead, in its own skills/ subfolder. That way Claude only reaches for it when it's actually working on something for that client, not every time it writes an email for anyone.

The same goes for what Claude creates. Content it drafts for you lands in the content folder. Work it does for a specific client lands inside that client's own folder. You give Claude a starting structure and a short map of what's where, and it files what it makes into the relevant department, not one shared pile everything gets dumped into.

Most of what goes into these folders already exists somewhere, a brand guide, voice notes, process docs. The work here is gathering and organising it into one system Claude can actually use.

worth checking today

Claude isn't lost, it's looking where you told it to look

This is the single most common thing that goes wrong once someone's actually using their AI Brain day to day, and it's rarely talked about.

A Scheduled Task saved its output inside Claude's own default folder instead of the actual project folder, because nobody had told it exactly where to save to, so it looked finished but the file was nowhere anyone could find it.
Someone's whole folder lived inside Downloads, and it wouldn't link at all. Downloads is treated differently by most software for security reasons, so it's one of the few places your AI Brain shouldn't actually live.
Someone had two copies of the same folder without realising it, one synced from Google Drive, one being edited directly. Claude was reading from the synced copy, so nothing typed into the real one ever showed up.
A finished document landed in the very top level of someone's Google Drive instead of inside the actual client folder, because the destination given was "save it to Drive" instead of the exact folder.

Each of these takes under a minute to fix once you know to check it. Go take a look at where your own tasks and outputs are actually landing today.

Claude, doing the work

Without a connector, Claude can only work with what you type or paste in. With one, it can reach into the tools you already use and read from them, or write to them, directly.

bonus: connectors

Claude, actually reaching into your tools

Each connector is like a unique plug. Every tool has its own shape, so each one needs its own connector to work with Claude. That's the real line between "Claude gave me an answer" and "Claude went and did the thing."

📁

Google Drive

Claude can read from and create files in a folder you choose.

✉️

Gmail

Read and draft directly from your inbox, without you copying anything across.

📅

Calendar

Claude can see what's actually on your schedule, not just what you tell it.

📝

Notion

Work directly with pages and databases you already keep there.

Find them by selecting Customise in the left menu panel, then Connectors, then Add, then browse connectors.

you're always in control

You decide exactly what it can do

Connecting a tool doesn't hand Claude the keys to everything in it. Permissions are set per tool and per action, not one blanket switch.

Real settings panel for a connected tool. Actions are grouped, here into Interactive tools and Read-only tools, and each group gets its own permission. Every individual action underneath can also be set on its own.

✅

Always allow

Claude just does it, no prompt each time.

🤚

Needs approval

Claude asks first, every time this action would run.

🚫

Blocked

Claude can't use this action at all, even if asked.

🔐
A simple rule that actually works
Set anything new to Needs approval while you're still learning what it does, especially anything that writes or changes something rather than just reads it. Loosen individual actions to Always allow once you've watched it work a few times and trust it.
One honest thing worth knowing before you assume too much: connectors often can read from a tool but not write to it, or reach some parts of a tool and not others. One real build hit exactly this, a connector could read and create new files in a client's system, but couldn't update an existing spreadsheet already in use there, which broke the workflow it was meant to support. Check what an action list actually shows before assuming a connector does everything a tool can do.

One setting most people never touch is the difference between Claude that already knows you and Claude that starts from zero, every single chat.

cowork global instructions

Set this once and everything gets a bit better

Open the Claude desktop app, go to Settings → Cowork, then click Edit next to Global Instructions.
Three things to start with.

1

Who you are and what you do

Your business, your role, the basics Claude should already know before any conversation starts.

2

Your shorthand and acronyms

So Claude doesn't have to ask what "the QBR deck" is, or whatever language is specific to how you work.

3

How you like output delivered

Format, length, tone, the standing preferences that should apply no matter what you're asking for.

not the same setting

Two different settings, not one

🎯
Global Instructions vs Instructions for Claude
Global Instructions live at Settings → Cowork, desktop app only, and apply to Cowork sessions specifically. Instructions for Claude (sometimes called Personal preferences) live at Settings → General, and apply everywhere, web, mobile and desktop, in every kind of chat.

Fill in Global Instructions only and your phone and regular web chats never see any of it. They're not two versions of the same thing, they cover different territory, and most people only ever find one of them.
the other half of this setup

There's a second setting working alongside this one: Memory

Instructions are the stable layer, things about you that don't change. Memory is the layer that accumulates on its own.

There are two separate toggles for it, under Settings → Capabilities → Memory.

On demand

Search and reference chats

Claude actively searches your past conversations when it's relevant. Available on paid plans (Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise).

Automatic

Generate memory from chat history

Claude quietly builds memory about your role, projects, working style and preferences as you go, in real time. Available on free plans too.

🧠
The two are meant to work together
If Memory is switched off, or was never checked, your Global Instructions are doing all the work alone with nothing backing them up between sessions, so it's worth being thorough there.

If Memory's on, it naturally fills in the gaps your instructions don't need to cover, since it keeps learning as you use Claude.

Most people have never been told these settings are meant to complement each other, or even confirmed both toggles are actually on.

Using Claude and actually benefiting from it are two very different things, and one of the best ways to start closing that gap today is Projects.

set up your first project

The department manager for one part of your business

Global instructions cover you, generally, everywhere. A Project covers what you're actually working on, one specific stream of work, with everything it needs to know about that part of your business.

🗂️
A dedicated workspace for one stream of work
Instead of 100's of disconnected long chats, a Project is a dedicated workspace for one body of work. Client Comms, Content, whatever department it covers. It already knows what it's for the moment you open it.
📦
Three things you set up, one Claude builds for you
Instructions, dedicated to this project.
Context, a folder or files Claude should work from.
Scheduled Tasks that belong to this project.
Memory, which Claude builds on its own as you work, genuinely dedicated so nothing leaks to other projects or standalone chats.
📋

Instructions

Specific to this project, not your general rules. What is this stream of work, who is it for, how should it be handled.

📁

Context

The actual folder or files Claude should reference for this project.

🧠

Memory (built for you)

What Claude learns from working inside this project, over time, automatically. You don't write this part.

🛠️
Setting up your first Project
Simpler than it sounds, four steps to a working department manager.
1

Create the Project

Name it for the department or stream of work it covers, e.g. "Client Comms" or "Content."

2

Tell Claude what it's for, let it draft the instructions

Describe what this Project is for and what's going into it, and Claude can write the first draft of its own instructions for you to check over.

3

Point it at a folder

Whatever files this stream of work actually needs Claude to reference. This is usually wherever your files already live, a folder in Google Drive, Dropbox, or right on your own device. You don't need to move anything first. Inside the Project, use the paperclip or Add Context option and link straight to that folder.

4

Start working inside it

Memory builds itself from here, every conversation adds to what it knows. Every chat you have inside this Project stays listed there too, so you can scroll back and find a past conversation instead of losing it in your main chat history.

✅
Better with every conversation
Outside a Project, every chat starts fresh except for your general instructions. Inside one, every conversation adds to what Claude already knows about that piece of your business. It gets more useful the more you use it, instead of starting from zero every time.
There's a practical bonus too. Files linked in as context are read directly, rather than pasted into every message. That uses far fewer tokens than copying the same document into chat again and again, so a Project is also a more efficient use of your usage limits.

a real distinction

Project or standalone?

There are two ways to set up a Scheduled Task. On its own (standalone), or inside a Project. The instructions work the same either way, what's different is how much the task actually knows when it runs.

⚙️

Standalone

Runs with only the instructions you gave it, nothing else. Fine for something simple and self-contained, checking one folder, pulling one report, the same steps every time.

🗂️

Inside a Project

Runs with the same instructions, plus that Project's own context, files and Memory behind it. Worth it the moment the task needs to make a judgement call, not just repeat a step.

💡
Why that difference actually matters
A Scheduled Task can't pause partway through and ask you a question. A weekly newsletter draft set up standalone can hit a decision it has no way to make, which voice to use, which client this update is actually about, and it has nowhere to turn. The same task set up inside a Project already has the brand voice, the client context and past decisions sitting there, so it can just make the call and keep going.

Simple version: nothing to judge, same steps every time, standalone is fine. Needs real context to make a call, build it inside a Project instead.

A well-built skill turns a task you re-explain every single time into one Claude just does properly the moment it recognises it, which is where the real productivity gain actually comes from.

build your first skill

Skills: the recipe card

A Skill is a recipe card. One task, done your way, every time. Don't try to pack three recipes into one card, and it can't be your mum's style of recipe with a dash of this and a splash of that. The more precise the card, the more consistent what comes back.

What's actually inside a skill

A skill isn't just one instruction. It's usually built from four kinds of things working together.

📋

Instructions (SKILL.md)

The brief that tells Claude what the skill does, when to use it, and how to do it.

🎨

Assets

Logos, brand templates, slide masters, fonts. The raw materials the skill uses to produce real-looking output.

📚

References

Examples of good output, style guides, clause libraries. This is how Claude learns what "good" looks like for this kind of work.

⚙️

Scripts

Small pieces of code Claude can run for the parts of the process that should happen exactly the same way every time.

Building one, step by step

You can build a skill through Skill Creator (below), or just start a new conversation and ask for one directly. Either way, this is what actually needs to happen.

1

Tell Claude the recurring task

Whatever you're tired of re-explaining, one task only, not three loosely related ones bundled together.

2

Let it interview you, and give it real examples

What good output looks like, what bad output looks like, who it's for, what should never happen. Point at real examples wherever you can, don't just describe them.

3

Correct it as you go

If something's off once it's built, just tell Claude what to change and ask it to update the skill. No need to start over.

Copy this in to build your first one

I do [describe the task] the same way every time, and I want to turn it into a Skill. Interview me about it one question at a time: what the finished result should look like, who it's for, and what should never happen. Get one good example and one bad example of the output from me before you write anything. Keep this skill to that one task only, don't fold in anything else it loosely relates to. Once you've got enough, build the skill and show it to me before we test it.

⚡
You don't call it up, Claude does
Skills are automatically used during a task, right when they're needed. You don't have to invoke one by name, you just start doing the work and Claude reaches for the right skill on its own, provided it's actually relevant to what you're doing.

⚠️ Don't hoard skills you're not using

Claude reads the top description of every skill you have listed to decide which one applies to your task, every single time, whether you end up using it or not. A long list of skills you built once and forgot about doesn't just clutter your Directory, it costs you tokens on every task. Toggle off anything you're not actively using.
