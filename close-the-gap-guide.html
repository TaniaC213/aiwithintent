Where your docs actually live

Anthropic's own research names this as one of three things separating businesses that get real value from AI and those that don't: reliable data in one place.

Your business knowledge

Before

Your business knowledge is scattered across five different apps. A doc here, a note there, a folder you forgot about. Claude can't use any of it properly, no matter how good the tool is.

  • Brand guide in Canva, voice notes in a Google Doc, processes in your head
  • You retype the same background information every time you open a new chat
  • Output feels generic because Claude genuinely doesn't know your business
After

One central place, sometimes called an AI Brain or AI-HQ, holding your voice, your brand, your business context and your processes. Claude has one clear place to work from instead of scattered fragments.

The trick isn't just dumping every file into one folder. It's setting it up the way you'd set up departments in a real business, one folder per area of your business, each holding only what belongs to that area. Claude can then be pointed at exactly the right department for whatever you're working on, instead of everything at once.

📁Your AI Brain/
├──About the Business.md← who you are, what you do, who it's for
├──Voice Profile.md← how you write, words you'd never use
├──📁brand/← logo, colours, fonts, style guide
├──📁clients/← one subfolder per client or project
├──📁content/← emails, social posts, drafts
└──📁operations/← processes, templates, how you work
  • One folder per department, not one giant pile of documents
  • Lives on your own device, not locked to any one AI tool
  • You plug the whole thing, or just one department, into whichever tool you choose, now or later

Do this today: list the 3-4 departments your business actually runs on, create a folder for each, and start moving what already exists into the right one.

Projects

The department manager for one part of your business. Point it at your AI Brain folder and it already knows your business the moment you open it.

🗂️
A scoped workspace for one stream of work
Global instructions cover you, generally, everywhere. A Project covers what you're actually working on, one specific part of your business, with everything it needs to know about that part.
📦
Three things you set up, one Claude builds for you
Instructions scoped to this project. Context, a folder or files Claude should work from. Scheduled Tasks that belong to this project. And Memory, which Claude builds on its own as you work, genuinely scoped so nothing leaks to other projects or standalone chats.
📋

Instructions

Specific to this project, not your general rules. What is this stream of work, who is it for, how should it be handled.

📁

Context

The actual folder or files Claude should reference, your AI Brain, or a subset relevant to this project.

🧠

Memory (built for you)

What Claude learns from working inside this project, over time, automatically. You don't write this part.

🛠️
Setting up your first Project
Simpler than it sounds, four steps to a working department manager.
1

Create the Project

Name it for the department or stream of work it covers, e.g. "Client Comms" or "Content."

2

Point it at your folder

Connect the relevant part of your AI Brain as its context.

3

Tell Claude what it's for, let it draft the instructions

Describe what this Project is for and what's going into it, and Claude can write the first draft of its own instructions for you to check over, rather than you starting from a blank box.

4

Start working inside it

Memory builds itself from here, every conversation adds to what it knows.

✅
Far beyond one conversation
Your AI Brain becomes the foundation everything else is built on, your Projects, your Skills, your Scheduled Tasks, any tool you plug it into going forward. Build it once, use it everywhere.

Do this today: create one Project, point it at a folder, and describe what it's for so Claude can help you draft the instructions.

Cowork Global Instructions

Set this once and everything you do in Claude gets a bit better from here on. Open the Claude desktop app, go to Settings → Cowork, then click Edit next to Global instructions.

1

Who you are and what you do

Your business, your role, the basics Claude should already know before any conversation starts.

2

Your shorthand and acronyms

So Claude doesn't have to ask what "the QBR deck" is, or whatever language is specific to how you work.

3

How you like output delivered

Format, length, tone, the standing preferences that should apply no matter what you're asking for.

While you're on this settings screen, check Cowork files at the top too. That's where Claude saves everything it creates, artifacts and Scheduled Task outputs included. Point it at a folder inside your AI Brain (or create one for it) so everything Claude produces lands somewhere you'll actually find it, instead of a default folder you'll forget about.

Do this today: open Settings → Cowork, fill in your global instructions using those three things as your starting list, and set your Cowork files location while you're there.

"Write in my voice" is too vague, Claude has to guess.

The glass-of-water test

How specific is specific enough? Here's a way to think about it.

Tap to learn more →
The analogy
Think of explaining to a robot how to bring you a glass of water. Where's the tap, do they turn it clockwise or anticlockwise, hot water or cold, fill to the top or half-filled?
Details matter. Otherwise Claude has to guess what you mean, and guessing is where inconsistent results come from.

Connectors: this is what ups the ante

Every one of the five ways above already moves you from basic to intermediate use. Connectors are what push it further again. This is the difference between Claude telling you what to do, and Claude actually doing it.

🔌
Claude, doing the work, not just chatting about it
Without a connector, Claude can only work with what you type or paste in. With one, it can actually reach into Google Drive, Gmail, your calendar or another tool and read from it or write to it directly. That's the real line between "Claude gave me an answer" and "Claude went and did the thing."
🔌

What a connector is

Called an MCP. Think of it like a unique plug, every tool has its own shape, and each one needs its own connector to work with Claude.

🗂️

Where to find them

Same Directory panel as Skills, under the Connectors tab, in Cowork's Customise menu.

🔐

Permissions

Adding one asks for permission to access that specific tool, so it only ever works with what you've approved, nothing more.

Do this today: once your first Scheduled Task is running, add one connector for the tool it actually needs to reach, and watch it do the work instead of just describing it.
