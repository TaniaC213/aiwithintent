What we heard

Three things that stood out from our call

Before we plan anything, it matters that you feel heard. Here's what we took away from your discovery call.

💭

ChatGPT knows you, but not your business

You've been using it for about a year and it's genuinely helped. But when you sit down to build a workbook for the women you work with, what comes back is close and still not quite yours. The gap isn't the tool. It's that nothing about your business is written down where the tool can read it.

"It's not personalised enough. I want to be able to get the ideas out there so I can actually start writing them. I don't want it to be all AI."
🌱

The business side is where it's actually going

You still want to work with women on the life coaching side, and that isn't going anywhere. But all three clients currently on your books are at the business foundation stage — no ABN yet, too scared to start. After thirteen years and three driving school start-ups, that's where your instinct keeps pulling.

"I know where I want it to go, but it's pushing me towards the business side of it."
🤲

It has to be easy for them, not just for you

The people you coach are already nervous. Anything you put in front of them has to feel simple or they won't follow through. So this isn't only about saving you time — it's about the client experience holding together from the first enquiry to the last session.

"You want to make it easier for me, but you want to make it easier for them as well. Because if you make it too complicated, they're not going to go through with it."

Your First Process — one, done properly
The business coaching client journey has several distinct processes inside it, and each one is a build of its own. Rather than do three things thinly, we pick the one causing you the most friction right now and define and automate that properly. The others can follow later, and the file architecture is built so they slot in.
📝

Option: client onboarding

The questions you need answered before session one, and what you send a new client in the first week — consistent every time, simple enough that someone nervous will finish it.

🎧

Option: session notes and pain points

You already record sessions. Point Claude at a transcript and it pulls out the sticking points and the client's own language, in the format you want, instead of you re-listening.

📊

Option: client pipeline

A place to see where each client sits across the six fortnightly sessions, set up in a tool Claude can read and write to.

1

You pick the one that hurts most

Have a think before the Brain Dump about which part of running a business coaching client eats the most time or slips most often. That's the one we build. We decide together on the call.

2

We map that process properly

Step by step, in your words: what happens, in what order, what you need from the client, where the information ends up. This is the part only you can supply — I can't invent your methodology.

3

It gets built, tested, and left room to grow

One process, documented and working, rather than three half-built. The structure is set up so the next process is a smaller job than this one was.

What changes in practice

Two moments in your month

This isn't about the setup doing your work for you. It's about what gets easier when there's a structure to start from.

📅

Turning your monthly blog into content

Now

You write the blog for the month. Then you have to work out what to pull from it for social, what becomes a reel, what topics you can talk to. Every piece starts with explaining your business to ChatGPT again, and what comes back is close but not quite you.

With your setup

You point Claude at the blog and say which arm it's for. It already has your voice, your offers and your audience, so what comes back is drafted in the right tone for the right people. This can then be used for social posts, reel angles, talking points.

You still edit it. You still decide what's worth saying. But you're starting from something that already sounds like you instead of from a blank box.

🤝

A new business foundation client signs on

Now

You hold the process in your head. You remember what you asked the last client because you did it recently. Notes from sessions sit wherever you took them. Nothing's broken — it works because there are three clients and you're across all three.

With your setup

The one process we build together is documented and repeatable. If you choose onboarding, the same questions get asked every time and you're not rebuilding it from memory. If you choose session notes, you drop a transcript in and get the sticking points back in your format.

One part of the journey stops living only in your head. Not the whole thing — but the part that costs you most, and a structure the next piece can be added to.

Two things to sort

Before the Brain Dump Call

None of these are urgent today, but all need to be settled before the build starts.

💳

A paid Claude account

Around $34 AUD a month. The free tier can't run the projects and scheduled tasks this setup relies on. Set it up in your own name — I never need your login details.

💻

The Claude desktop app

Download it from claude.com/download and follow the steps for your device. On a Mac you'll need macOS 11 (Big Sur) or newer. It also runs a small virtual machine on your computer, so give yourself around 25GB of free disk space and 8GB of RAM. Worth checking before you book — go to the Apple menu, About This Mac, then Storage.

🗃️

A client management tool

Have a look at Notion, Airtable and ClickUp before the call. All three let Claude read and write. You don't need to commit — just have a feel for which one you'd tolerate opening.

📤

Your assets in the shared folder

I'll send a Google folder when you book. Drop in brand guidelines, lead magnets, workbooks, writing samples, any process notes. The more that's in there, the closer the build lands.
