Two businesses in one

You're running two substantial operations side by side. There's the event management arm, with around 600 exhibitors across your shows each year, working through a cycle that never really stops: selling space, bookings and communications, electrical forms, then the delivery of the shows themselves. Then there's a full in-house marketing function: social media across three cities, copywriting, graphic design, video and a television program on Channel 7.

The patterns I noticed

  • The same work happens three times. Newsletters, social content and exhibitor communications run per show, largely by hand, for Sydney, Adelaide and Perth.
  • Your systems don't talk to each other. The custom database has served you for a decade, but getting information between it, your invoicing and your website involves manual re-entry that eats hours.
  • Repetitive exhibitor admin. Booking confirmations, form chasing and updates for hundreds of exhibitors follow the same patterns every cycle, and each one is still handled individually.
  • A capable team carrying it manually. You, Ann and your accounts manager each hold a big piece of how everything runs, mostly in your heads and your inboxes.

None of this is a criticism, by the way. A business that has grown steadily for this long, by adding what each season needed, is a business that works. What you're feeling now is simply that the tools have jumped ahead, and it's the right moment to let them carry some of the load.

Where AI could support Premiere Events

You mentioned some genuinely exciting possibilities on our call, including the production-system integration you saw at Cub Campers. I want to be straight with you about the order I'd recommend, because it's the difference between AI that sticks and AI that gets abandoned.

The foundation comes first. Your team hasn't worked with Claude yet, so the first build needs to deliver visible wins quickly, teach everyone how these tools think, and build the confidence that makes everything after it possible. Complex integrations built before that foundation tend to become expensive things nobody quite trusts.

Points to note

A few practical things worth knowing before we begin, so there are no surprises later.

Your computers

Claude's desktop app needs Windows 10 or newer, or macOS 11 (Big Sur) or newer. Any reasonably recent office computer will be fine. Worth a quick check across the team's machines before a build.

A Claude subscription, held by you

Everything I build lives in your accounts and belongs to you, which means the subscription is yours too. For a team like yours the natural fit is the Claude Team plan:

  • Priced per person in US dollars: US$25 each per month (or US$20 on annual billing)
  • The plan has a five-seat minimum, so a four-person team still pays for five seats. That's roughly A$190 per month at current exchange rates, and entering your ABN at checkout means GST isn't added
  • What you get for it: central billing and admin, everyone managed in one place, higher usage than individual plans, and your business content isn't used for model training by default

The lighter alternative is individual Claude Pro accounts at US$20 per person per month (about A$30 each), which works well but leaves each account managed separately. The roadmap will include my recommendation either way, and I can help you set up whichever you choose.

How we'll work

The audit itself happens online, because recording the calls means I can go back through every detail rather than relying on notes. When we get to a build, I'm very happy to come to you for the setup and installation session so the whole team learns together.

Other tools, only if they earn it

Depending on what the roadmap surfaces, one or two purpose-specific tools might be worth a small subscription, for example a social scheduling tool for posting across all your platforms from one place. My rule is simple: nothing gets recommended unless the audit shows it clearly paying for itself, and anything we do add is chosen so your setup stays yours, not locked to one vendor. Some ideas, like gathering exhibitor social activity, also need checking against the platforms' own rules, and that honesty check is part of the audit.
